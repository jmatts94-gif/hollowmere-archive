PROLOGUE

The First

Every lantern casts a shadow, and every shadow knows its lantern.

Before towns had light or life, before the roads had tales of old and new, before the first burrow door was ever carved, there were only the First.

The Badger. The Owl. The Toad. The Rat. One of every critter, vast and old, wise, and stoic. These were the flash of light at the beginning.

But nothing that bright can stay whole for ever. In time, when critters had built their towns and lit their lanterns, nearly every one of the First was split, like light diffusing through each stained-glass pane that lined every burrow. One half shone, the other cast a shadow, both intertwined at a singular point. The critters of this world never learned the names who came before. They only spoke of them in whispers. Those Above. Those Below.

Fractions of those whispers can be seen in the mere, or reflected off puddles created by the changing cycles. Those old enough to remember tales of old will know not to stare too long. You can hear some of those critters across the land in Hollowmere telling stories and fables of Those Above and Those Below. Nobody truly understands what they mean, or why they repeat certain phrases over and over again, like junebugs bumping into the night lanterns, time and time again. One thing is certain: their tales hold weight in the ears of those who choose to listen.

“Never stare into the mere for too long. You never know what is staring back.”

Every one of the First still has their other half. One walks in the light, above the water. The other waits in its reflection, deep below, where the light has never reached. They are bound together, like a critter and its shadow on a summer’s day. This is the story of one of those shadows.
